How the computer plays

No magic, no cheating: it only sees the same board you do. Here's what goes on in its head.

Easy: the daydreamer

Most of the time (6 moves out of 10) it's off in its own world and just drops its O on any free square. The other 4 times it snaps awake and plays its very best move. So it can surprise you, but you can definitely beat it!

Unbeatable 3×3: the fortune teller

Before every move it imagines every way the rest of the game could go: each move, each reply, all the way to the end. A 3×3 board only has a few hundred thousand possible games, and a computer can flip through them all in a blink.

It assumes you'll play perfectly too, and picks the move that works out best for it anyway. That's why the best you can do is a draw. (Grown-ups call this minimax.)

Hard 4×4 and 5×5: the speed thinker

Bigger boards have billions and billions of possible games, far too many to imagine them all. So the computer gets a timer: about a third of a second per move. It looks 1 move ahead, then 2, then 3, going as deep as it can before the timer rings.

When it can't see the ending, it guesses who's winning by checking every possible line:

  • A line with only O's in it is good news, and every extra O makes it ten times better.
  • A line with only X's in it is bad news, by the same amount.
  • A line with both X and O is blocked, so it's worth nothing.

It also tries the middle squares first, and skips moves it can already tell are worse (that trick is called alpha-beta pruning). Clever, but it can't see everything, so set it a trap that's further ahead than it can look!

Hard Ultimate: the big-picture planner

The same timer trick, but it keeps score its own way:

  • Winning a small board: worth a lot.
  • Two won small boards in a row on the big board: worth even more.
  • Two in a row inside a small board: a little bonus.
  • Sending you to a finished board: it hates that, because then you get to play anywhere!
